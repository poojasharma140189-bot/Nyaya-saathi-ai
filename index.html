CASE GUIDE · INDIA

Buying a home or property

Use this general checklist to prepare for a purchase. Documents differ for resale and builder properties, and requirements vary by state and property type.

Documents to check

Tick items as you collect or verify them. Ask an independent property lawyer to review the originals and local records before paying a substantial amount or signing.

    Typical steps

    1. Confirm the details. Identify the exact property, seller, total price, payment schedule, included fixtures and any promised handover date. Budget for applicable taxes, stamp duty, registration and professional fees.
    2. Verify title and authority to sell. Have an independent property lawyer review the title chain, the seller's ownership and authority, and relevant local land or registration records. Check for mortgages, liens, disputes, unpaid dues and restrictions. An encumbrance search alone does not establish clear title.
    3. Check approvals and the property itself. Confirm the land use and applicable building, layout and occupancy approvals with the relevant local authorities. Inspect the property and verify its area and boundaries against the records.
    4. Check project registration where applicable. For a covered project, look up its registration and disclosures on the state RERA authority's official website. Confirm which rules apply to this project and transaction.
    5. Agree terms in writing. Get the sale agreement reviewed before signing or paying. It should accurately record the parties, property, price, payment milestones, possession, included items and consequences if obligations are not met.
    6. Complete the transfer. Arrange payment through traceable methods. Pay the applicable stamp duty and register the sale deed with the appropriate Sub-Registrar, following state requirements. Obtain the registered deed and payment receipts.
    7. After registration. Take possession against a written handover record, collect keys and original documents due to you, transfer utilities where applicable, and apply to update relevant municipal or land records. Mutation or a tax-record update is not by itself proof of ownership.
    Before you pay or sign: Do not rely only on photocopies, a broker's assurances, a bank loan approval or a builder's marketing material as proof of clear title. Ask a qualified local property lawyer to review the particular transaction.
